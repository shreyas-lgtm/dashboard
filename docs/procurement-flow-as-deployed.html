The one-sentence version

A requester files a Google Form and chases their own lead; the lead's dropdown on the sheet creates an Asana card routed to one of three procurement people; procurement drags that card across a board and the sheet follows within ten minutes — except that the card cannot pass Ordered until a price has been typed into the sheet.

02 Step by step, with real latencies

00

Requester submits the Google Form

Within a second or two a script assigns the next PR_ID from a counter — PR-2026-1528, continuing the old form's sequence. Nothing else happens, and the requester receives no confirmation, so they do not learn their own PR ID unless they open the sheet.

01

Requester chases their lead; the lead sets the dropdown

No notification goes to anyone. The lead opens the sheet, finds the row, and picks Approved, Rejected or Re-verify. This is the step with no clock on it — on the old form, 10 requests marked "Critical — within 24 hours" sat undecided for a median of 11 days.

Occasionally someone senior fills Final Approval instead. That outranks the lead's cell and is written back into it. It is usually blank, and blank changes nothing.

Who acts
Team lead, chased by requester

Hours to weeks — unmeasured

02

The card appears in Asana, already assigned

Seconds after the dropdown changes, a card lands in Pending, routed by Product type: Mechanical to Kiran, Electrical to Abish, Office supplies to Syed, anything else to Abish. The description carries requester, item, quantity, part number, product type, urgency, preferred vendor, justification and link. The task URL and GID are written back to the sheet row.

Procurement drags the card to Quotation Awaited

Up to ten minutes later the sheet's Order Status reads Quotation Awaited. Any comments they left on the card are mirrored into the sheet at the same time.

04

The quotation arrives — and procurement leaves Asana

This is the awkward step. The price is only entered in the responses sheet, so whoever owns the ticket has to open the sheet, find the row for that PR ID, and type the number. Vendor comparison happens off-system entirely.

Who acts
Procurement, in the sheet
Friction

If skipped
The card cannot pass Ordered
05

Ordered — or bounced back

On the next poll, if a price exists the sheet records Ordered. If it does not, the card is dragged back to where it came from and a comment explains why. Asana cannot block a drag, only react to one, so the card visibly sits in the wrong column for up to ten minutes first.

Ordered Date is stamped only if the sheet happens to have that column; it is not created automatically.

Who acts
Procurement drags; script judges
Latency
Up to 10 minutes either way
Visible oddity
The card snaps backwards on its own
06

Handed Over, Rework, or Cancelled

Handed Over is also price-gated. Rework emails the requester to recheck the request — the only status change they hear about. Cancelled is where a rejected request lands, unless it had already reached Ordered or Handed Over, in which case the card is deliberately left alone and a human is alerted instead.

Procurement

email on Rework only

03 What each person actually experiences

Requester

Files it, then chases

  • Submits the form. No confirmation, no PR ID.
  • Must find and chase their own lead — nothing prompts the lead.
  • Cannot see status: no Asana seat, and the sheet holds everyone's requests and prices.
  • Hears from the system only on Rejected, Re-verify, or Rework.
  • Learns the item arrived by physically receiving it.
  • No way to cancel or amend a submitted request.
Team lead

Two clicks, if they remember

  • Nothing tells them a request is waiting.
  • Opens the sheet, finds the row, picks a dropdown value.
  • Approving is instant and needs no follow-up.
  • Approves without knowing the cost — price arrives later, from the quotation.
  • Can reverse a decision later; the card follows.
Procurement (×3)

Lives on the board, visits the sheet

  • Cards arrive pre-assigned and pre-filled; no triage needed.
  • Works by dragging; can reassign to each other freely and it sticks.
  • Must switch to the sheet to enter each price.
  • Sees nothing before approval, so cannot plan for long-lead items.
  • Nowhere structured to record PO number, chosen vendor, or delivery date.
  • Due dates come from urgency, not lead time — so most cards look overdue immediately.

The three silent ones matter most

A pasted approval, an edited form response, and a deleted row all produce a sheet that looks correct and an Asana board that quietly disagrees with it. None of them alerts anybody. Over two months at ~19 tickets a week, expect a handful — worth a weekly eyeball comparing row count against card count.

05 What has never been tested

229 assertions cover the logic that can be checked offline: routing across all six Product type options, the price gate, the approval override matrix, section reading, comment formatting, and column resolution against both the old and the expected new header rows.

Not one Asana API call has ever run. The connector is disabled in the session this was built in, so the payload shapes for task creation, section moves, comments and pagination are written from the API documentation and have never met a real workspace. That is the single largest remaining risk, and it is resolved by walking one ticket end to end.

Before trusting it, do this once

  • testConnection() → token valid
  • checkSheetMapping() → every field resolves, especially price and productType
  • ensureSections() → six board columns exist
  • verifyRouting() → all three owners resolve to Asana users
  • seedPrIdCounter(1527) → numbering continues rather than restarting
  • Submit a real request, approve it, drag it to Ordered without a price, confirm it bounces, add the price, confirm it sticks
